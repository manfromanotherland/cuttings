Cuttings helps you collect articles, images, videos, and quotes that inspire you. Everything you save lives in a plain folder you control, on iCloud Drive, Dropbox, Google Drive, or wherever you like. No accounts, no servers, just files.

Here is a quick tour to get you started.

## Saving from the web

Install the Cuttings browser extension from this project. Use its toolbar button to save a full article, or right-click an image, video, or text selection and choose **Add to Cuttings**. Every card keeps the page it came from, and articles are cleaned into Markdown before they reach your collection.

## Your collection

Everything you save lands on one visual board. Use the toolbar to search, filter by kind or tag, change the sort order, or focus on your favorites.

Click any card to open it. Use **←** and **→** to move through nearby ideas without reaching for the mouse.

## Highlights

Select text in any article, right-click, and choose **Highlight**. Everything you highlight gathers in one place, which you can open from the toolbar or with **⌘⇧H**. Your highlights are saved right next to the article, so they stay with you even outside the app.

## Search

Press **⌘K** to search. It looks through everything you have saved and puts the closest matches first. You can still change the sort order while you search.

## Tags

With an item open, press **⌘⇧T** to add tags. Tags help you group ideas by project, topic, or anything else that makes sense to you.

## Favorites

Use the heart on any item to keep it close. The heart in the toolbar switches between your full collection and just your favorites.

## Typography and appearance

Open **Settings → Typography** to choose how article text looks, including its font, size, width, and spacing. Switch between light and dark under **Settings → Appearance**.

## Keyboard shortcuts

Press **⌘/** to see every shortcut. Most actions have one, so you can move through your collection without lifting your hands from the keyboard.

Everything you save stays on your computer as a simple file that belongs to you. You can move it, back it up, or open it anywhere. I would keep your collection in a folder that syncs to the cloud, like Dropbox, iCloud, or Google Drive, whichever you already use.
